# Story

Scripted story scenes: what starts them, and any characters that only exist for them. The scenes are cutscenes (see [the Cutscenes README](../Cutscenes/README.md)). Their data lives in this folder's `Resources/Cutscenes` and `Resources/GameEvents`.

## Prologue (the start of New Game)

The 3D prologue from the game design document opens every new game: it is the `prologue` step at the start of the opening cutscene (`opening_arrival`, see [the Cutscenes README](../Cutscenes/README.md)). START goes straight into it as the menu begins to close, and when it ends the star's light gathers onto the player in the dark forest. In a game, Esc twice skips it, like a cutscene. It can still be watched on its own with **Lumenfall > Story > Preview Prologue**: outside Play Mode this enters Play Mode and starts it over the title menu, and in Play Mode it starts at once. As a preview, Esc (or **Stop Prologue Preview**) stops it. Either way it saves nothing and changes no game state.

About 53 seconds, in nine shots:

1. A two-lane highway at night, tracking alongside the student's car (left lane, driver on the right). "11:42 PM". The phone's notifications start stacking in the top right: uni deadlines, the shift manager, the group chat, Mum, the bank.
2. Inside, from the passenger seat: the student's face in the light of the phone on its dash mount, notifications arriving faster. *"Just get home. Just get home."* *"Why is everything due on the same day?"*
3. Outside: the car swerves left onto a dirt road and brakes to a stop, with a tyre squeal and a shake. *"Nope. Not tonight."*
4. The engine cuts, the door opens (with its chime), the student steps out and throws the phone into the field through the headlights; every notification on screen breaks apart.
5. They scream at the field (the shout shakes letter by letter), then slump. The phone buzzes once more, far off in the grass.
6. They walk back and lean on the car, looking up. Crickets. *"When did I last actually look up?"* *"Huh. They're kind of pretty out here."*
7. Low beside them, the camera tilts up over the car roof into the stars, and one star brightens and twinkles.
8. Wide: the star dives at them, lighting the car and the field gold. *"...Is that one getting bigger?"*
9. Close on their face, lit from above. *"Wait. Stars don't do th-"* White, then ink, then black, and the forest arrival follows straight on. There is no title card: the game's own title is the menu's.

- **Pieces.** `PrologueScript` is the timeline as data (shots, notifications, lines, beats and the car's drive), covered by `PrologueScriptTests`. `PrologueSet` builds the set in code at `(4000, 0, 4000)` on layer 31: the road, dirt road, guide posts, trees, hills, fence, a dome of stars with a band of the galaxy and a moon, a low-poly car built from primitives, and the student: the player's own model (the Chibi Witch the player prefab uses, through `PrologueArt` in Resources) before they ever reach the academy, with the witch hat taken off and the robe recoloured through the model's customisable material into a grey-blue hoodie, dark jeans and off-white trim, and the player's own blonde hair (the material's default is pink). It plays the NPC controller's Idle, Walk, Angry, Yelling and Sad states by name, which its humanoid rig takes. Without that art it falls back to a background NPC model recoloured into a hoodie. `PrologueScene` poses it on unscaled time, frames each shot, plays the generated sounds (`PrologueAudio`, plus the opening star's own fall and impact) and draws the overlay.
- **Sitting and getting out.** The NPC model has no sitting animation, so after the animator poses it standing, `PrologueScene` swings its thighs forward, bends its knees and, while driving, reaches its arms to the wheel; the hips stay put, so the seat puts them on the cushion. The model's head is most of its height, so the car's cabin is open (floor, seats, dashboard, low sides and the driver's door) and its roof is raised to the student's measured sitting height. At the stop they turn to the open door and hop down, then walk out past the door's edge; they lean on the back of the car, clear of the roof.
- **Over the menu.** Its camera renders into a texture drawn with IMGUI at depth -1001 over solid black, in front of every overlay, and it takes clicks and keys so nothing behind reacts. The title menu is not drawn and does not tick while it plays; when it ends, the menu fades in and plays its own opening title from the start. It hushes the music (`CutscenePlayer.MusicHushed`). While it plays, every other light in the scene is switched off and the ambient light, fog, reflections and sun are set for night; all of it is put back when it stops.
- **Reduced Motion** turns off the camera shakes, the notification shake and the shout's letter jitter.
- **Not decided yet:** the seated pose is bent by hand rather than animated, the car is placeholder primitives (Kenney's CC0 car kit would match the furniture already used), and the timings, lines and notification text are first drafts.

## The dark forest (`ForestNight`)

The walk from where the player wakes to the academy gate, as the design document has it: dark, with a light leading the way.

- **Night.** From the moment the opening's light gathers until the gate scene has played, `westForest` is night. The sun is dimmed, the ambient light turns blue-black and fog closes in. It is darkest at the spawn (row 6) and lifts as the player nears the gate (row 46, `ForestNight.Darkness`), where a warm glow over the gate shows the academy. A soft light around the player keeps the ground readable. Everything it changes is recorded first and put back as the night lifts: on reaching campus, on leaving the forest, under the title menu, and while the prologue plays (`PrologueScene.Starting`), since the prologue sets its own night. Coming back after the gate, the forest is as it always was.
- **The wisp.** It appears as the player wakes (with the first-thoughts dialogue, which now notices it and wonders whether this is how people die in horror movies) and darts six cells ahead of them along a `GridPathfinder` path to `(17,42)`, just short of Kepler at the gate, catching up quickly whenever they move, so it always leads. When they stop it waits there, swaying a little further on and back as if beckoning. It chimes once when it first appears, and sheds motes. When the gate scene starts, it rises into the academy's light and fades. There is no direction marker or extra card: the onboarding hint card is retitled "Follow the light through the dark forest" and still lists the controls.
- **The orange tree.** Two or three cells to the side of the first path the wisp finds, nine cells along it, stands a small tree of glowing oranges (built from primitives, with a blocker). **Look** at it (`forest_orange_tree` in `Resources/GameEvents/forest_walk.json`): the player is starving, picks one, and it is an apple.

## Late to orientation (the academy gate)

`LateToOrientationScene` runs the arrival at the academy gate, at the end of the walk through the dark forest.

- **Who is there.** Until the scene has played, Prof. Kepler stands in `westForest` at `(19,46)`, beside the gate to campus (portal `P.F03.A01`, three cells wide from x 16, entered at `(17,48)`). Kepler is the `prof_kepler` record in `NpcCatalog.asset`, with the `Teacher_01` prefab and no dialogue of their own, spawned through `StoryNpc`. The late student is a story extra, not a catalog character: `GateCast` (in Resources) holds their model (`Student_06`) and the scene spawns them from a record made in code, so they have no introduction and no journal entry. There is no queue at the gate.
- **Trigger.** The first time the player reaches row 41 of the forest or beyond, anywhere across the map (the wisp leads them there, and the forest's other campus portals are on these rows too), with no cutscene, dialogue or menu open, the cutscene `late_to_orientation` plays. It is play-once and unskippable.
- **The scene:**
  1. Kepler shouts from the gate (speaker "Distant voice", since the two have not met): orientation started ages ago, come over quickly. The inner voice wonders whether the afterlife has an orientation.
  2. The game walks the player to two cells in front of Kepler (`(19,44)`).
  3. Kepler introduces themselves: the player has already missed orientation, on their very first day. Then: "Your letter of admission, please." The player has none ("Uhhhhhhhh."), so they are trespassing.
  4. Right then, the late student appears down the trail and runs for the gate, as it happens, shouting for them to hold the gate and that she is SO late. Her lines are speech bubbles over her head (the `say` step), with no dialogue box or click, and they go the moment she stops: she only speaks on the move. She trips a tile from the player with a quick "Waaah!", to the dog door's jingle (`Audio/Dog/Dog Door`, the `sound` step), and her letter flutters through the air into the player's hands. She says nothing more once she is still. There is no countdown.
  5. Kepler asks why they didn't show it earlier, takes it (it flutters over), and cannot read the smudged name, so the player gives theirs (the name prompt; blank gives Sol). "{player}. There, you're on the list. Enjoy the next few years!" Pointing: in you get, head straight down to what's left of orientation. The inner voice grumbles about being sent from one school straight into another.
  6. The flag `story.orientation.kepler_met` is set and the player is hurried through the gate. A short fade to black and back, with nothing written over it, and they are on campus, having already missed orientation, so Kit's "you missed the ceremony" follows on.
- **Afterwards.** Kepler is removed as soon as the cutscene ends and is never spawned again here; they are meant to return elsewhere later. The late student stays outside the gate at `(15,47)`, sobbing (`gate_late_student_after`): they HAD a letter.
- **NPC steps.** The late student's run, trip and sob, Kepler's point and the letter use the cutscene steps `npcWalk`, `npcPose` and `letter` (see [the Cutscenes README](../Cutscenes/README.md)). The late student is spawned only once the cutscene sets `story.gate.late_student_runs`, so they never stand waiting down the trail.
- **Saves.** Autosave waits during the scene, and the flag is saved with the GameEvents state. A save in the forest beyond row 41 from before the scene plays it as soon as it loads. Kepler and the late student, and their blockers, are removed while the startup menu is open, as with every `StoryNpc`. If a save leaves the player on Kepler's cell, Kepler appears once the player steps off it.

Kepler's shout has no portrait, because they are far away when it plays. Once they introduce themselves, the speaker is "Prof. Kepler", which matches the catalog name, so the dialogue box shows Kepler's portrait.

## Lay of the land (arriving on campus)

`LayOfTheLandScene` runs the arrival on campus, straight after Kepler's gate scene.

- **Kit.** Kit is the student at the campus entrance: `campus_student`, renamed from "Campus student" in the catalog, the campus prefab, `npcs.json` and the journal. Kit stands at `(24,13)` in the campus prefab; the catalog's `(32,21)` is out of date, so the scene uses Kit's live position.
- **The call.** On campus, once `story.orientation.kepler_met` is set and `story.orientation.kit_called` is not, with no cutscene, dialogue or menu open, the cutscene `kit_call` plays. In practice that is the moment the player comes through the forest gate. Kit calls the player over, the player's inner voice notes that at least this stranger is smiling, and the flag `story.orientation.kit_called` is set. Control returns, and the player walks over themselves. Nothing walks them.
- **The tour.** Once the call has played, the first time the player talks to Kit (the talk key, `E` by default, within Kit's usual talk range), the cutscene `kit_lay_of_the_land` plays instead of Kit's usual conversation. The scene runs before NPC input, so it takes that press first. After the tour, talking to Kit opens Kit's usual choice-based courtyard conversation again. Both cutscenes are play-once and unskippable.
- **The tour's content:**
  1. Kit introduces themselves and gives the lay of the land: the courtyard with its fountain in the middle (the place to head back to if lost), the Main Academy Building (reception, the main portal, the staff), the Arcane Workshop (relics and machines), the Dormitory (student rooms and common spaces), the Library, building signs by every door, and the forest back through the gate. The directions are landmarks rather than left and right, because the camera's diagonal makes screen directions differ from the map, and older map data disagree about which side each building is on.
  2. Kit says the player missed the orientation ceremony, and that the headmistress, Professor Bann, is still by the fountain: go and speak to her right away. The flag `story.orientation.kit_met` is set and control returns. There is no hint card or marker.
- **Prof. Bann** is the `prof_bann` record (`Teacher_03`, campus `(26,42)`, by the fountain). She is spawned through `StoryNpc` from the moment `story.orientation.kepler_met` is set, and the player talks to her with E.
  - Her conversation `bann_orientation`: the player has missed orientation entirely, and she will organise a student guide to go over it with them tomorrow. For now they should report to their dorm and get their room sorted. Today is for exploring and speaking to fellow students, so she suggests the room first, then whatever they please. The player's inner voice decides that, for the afterlife, this isn't half bad. Her last line sets `story.orientation.bann_met`. She does not say where the dorm is; there is no hint card or marker.
  - Talking to her again gives the short `bann_reminder` ("Your dorm first, {player}…") instead. Her first node triggers the repeatable event `bann_remind_if_met`, which swaps in the reminder once the flag is set.
- **Kit's everyday conversation** (`ambient_courtyard_student`, after the tour): "Hey, {player}! Settling in all right?", with two choices. "Can you remind me of the campus buildings and areas?" repeats the landmark tour: the fountain, the Main Academy Building, the Arcane Workshop, the Dormitory and Library, the forest back through the gate, and the building signs. "Bye!" gets "See you around!". Kit's two one-line courtyard chats (`_2`, `_3`) are unchanged, apart from the speaker now being Kit.

## Meeting people

`NpcIntroductions` keeps track of whom the player has met. Until someone has introduced themselves, the dialogue name ribbon and the journal show them as `???`. Their frame, colour and portrait still show.

- **First conversation.** The first time the player talks to an NPC, the NPC plays their introduction before anything else. The introduction is `intro_<person>` in `Resources/GameEvents/introductions.json`. They give their name, ask the player's, and the player answers through a choice, "I'm {player}.". They then go straight on to the conversation they would otherwise have had, such as a quest offer, without the dialogue box closing.
- **The name shows from the line where they give it.** That line's `SetFlag` sets `npc.met.<person>`, which is saved with the game. Later conversations skip the introduction and use the player's name as usual.
- **People with several records** count as one person. The three Cinna records are all `cinna_cirra`, and the speaker name "Cinna Cirra" is matched to that person too.
- **Scripted first meetings** set the flag in their own dialogue. Prof. Kepler is `???` while calling the player over, and is named on "I'm Professor Kepler" (`kepler_late`/`late`); Kepler's name prompt at the gate is how the player first gives their name. Kit is `???` while shouting (`kit_call`). The tour (`kit_lay_of_the_land`) now opens with Kit's name and a question for the player's, before the lay of the land. Saves made before this existed count Kepler and Kit as met through the scenes' own flags.
- **Prof. Bann, the headmistress,** is known to everyone from the start, and needs no introduction.
- **General conversation once met.** After the first meeting, talking to someone opens their `chat_<person>` dialogue (`Resources/GameEvents/conversations.json`). They greet the player by name and offer "How's your day going?", "Tell me about yourself.", one topic of their own and "Bye!". Each answer returns to "Anything else?" with the same topics, until the player says goodbye. Their quest dialogue still comes first when it is what they need to say: a quest to offer (its prerequisite met), a task done but not yet handed in (its "report" choice is available), or a quest finished whose thanks the player has not heard yet (`npc.quest-thanked.<quest>`, set once any of their quest conversations ends with that quest complete). While a quest is simply under way, their chat adds "About what you asked me to do...", which hands over to the quest dialogue for a reminder. Kit keeps their own chat (`ambient_courtyard_student`). Prof. Bann keeps her story lines.
- **An NPC with no introduction** counts as met from their first conversation, so a new NPC is never stuck as `???`.
- **An unnamed player** (only possible on saves from before the name system) is asked for their name by the `ask_player_name` cutscene before an introduction.

The introductions' and conversations' lines are placeholders, to be rewritten.

## Settling in (end of Chapter 1)

`SettlingInScene` runs the rest of Chapter 1, from the dormitory to the end of the chapter: a wild goose chase for the room key that ends in a glitch.

1. **The key is with a dormmate.** Once Prof. Bann has spoken to the player (`story.orientation.bann_met`), talking to Marigold at the dorm reception plays `dorm_room_not_ready` instead of Marigold's usual chat, after Marigold's introduction if this is their first meeting. Their room is sorted, but she has already handed the key to one of their four dormmates; she does not say which. It sets `story.dorm.room_not_ready` (the name is older than the key hunt). Until then Bann keeps her orientation lines and her reminder about the dorm; from then on she chats like anyone else (`chat_prof_bann`). Marigold's chat offers "Who has my key again?" until the key is handed over.
2. **Exploring.** The player explores and solves the puzzles. Nothing guides them. The first time the player talks to Rowan or Thistle, a short scene plays at the desk first (`dorm_no_rooms`, setting `story.dorm.no_rooms_scene`): Rowan pleads for a room, Thistle says tough luck, and Marigold asks Thistle not to laugh. Then whoever the player spoke to carries on as usual, introduction included. The dorm's room doors all say the devs are sleeping and to visit later. Once the room-ready thought has played (step 3), the joke pays off and stays that way: Rowan never got a room, and has moved onto the lounge floor at `(27,7)` (reconciled in code each time the dorm is placed or the story state changes, with his obstacle), where he says so (`dorm_rowan_homeless`). Talking to Thistle then gives only the player's thought that he is about to burst into tears of laughter, and Marigold telling him not to laugh (`dorm_thistle_laughing`). The desk scene no longer plays by then. Starting a New Game or loading an earlier save returns him and his blocker to the authored reception cell before the player is placed, even when the dorm instance is cached. Live moves wait if the player is standing on or moving into his destination. His authored reception pose faces Thistle across the desk; as with other NPCs, he may turn to a nearby player.
3. **Asking the dormmates.** Each of the four dormmates (`SettlingInScene.Dormmates`) is asked about the key once their last favour is done: Stella after *String Them Together*, Cinna Cirra after *Cold Feet, Warm Idea*, Orio after *Patch Notes* and Katrina after *Three Lost Echoes*. The question (`key_ask_<person>`) follows straight on, in the same dialogue box, from whichever dialogue the dormmate speaks in as the favour wraps up, so for Cinna Cirra it follows the ice trial's solved line. If that is missed (a favour finished before Marigold sent the player, or an older save), it is the dormmate's story turn the next time they are spoken to, once their thanks has been heard. Each says they do not have it and someone else must, and sets `story.key.asked.<person>`. Before Marigold has sent the player, nobody is asked. The hint card counts the favours done and the dormmates asked, and its arrow leads to the next dormmate not yet asked.
4. **Back to the receptionist.** Once every Chapter 1 puzzle is solved (the six quests in `SettlingInScene.ChapterQuestIds`), all four dormmates have been asked and nothing else is on screen, the cutscene `room_ready_thought` plays the player's inner voice: that's all four, none of them has it, so they will have to ask the receptionist. It sets `story.dorm.room_ready_thought`. The player then walks back themselves.
5. **The glitch, and the key.** Talking to Marigold then plays `chapter1_room_ready`:
   1. Marigold asks whether they found it; the player says none of their dormmates have it (`dorm_key_glitch_ask`).
   2. The music and ambience hush.
   3. "Oh." "It was here the whole time." Then, on "Why didn't you ask for it?" (`dorm_key_glitch`), Marigold's glitch aura (`GlitchAura`, below) breaks out at full strength with its drone, through the `setaura` action on that line, and her name tag flickers into Wingdings. Nothing shows the aura before this line.
   4. The camera creeps in on the player over seven seconds while they turn, slowly, over five seconds, to face the camera, their head lifting to look straight into it, and hold it.
   5. The picture freezes and tears (`glitch` "tear"), and cuts to black. Behind the black, the aura, the stare and the camera are released and the music returns.
   6. Tracking lines roll over the screen as it fades back in (`glitch` "rewind"). Marigold, normal, hands over the key as if nothing happened (`dorm_room_ready`, setting `story.dorm.key_received`). Nobody remarks on it.
   7. The player is walked to the third room door at `(17,17)`, the screen fades to black and "End of Chapter 1" appears, `story.chapter1.complete` is set, and the screen fades back on the player at their door. The game then saves and returns to the main menu.

   With Reduced Motion, the aura stays as a still red and blue outline, the tear is a slow fade into deep violet, the rewind is sound only, and the name tag stays in Wingdings without flickering.
6. **Afterwards.** Loading that save puts the player back at their door, with no further story. Marigold is herself again, with no aura, and her chat offers "Thanks for the room key!" in place of "Who has my key again?".

### Glitch aura

`GlitchAura` draws a glitchy aura around a character, built in code like the spell effects, in a deep violet, magenta-red and electric-blue palette: red and blue copies of their current pose (`Resources/GlitchGhost.shader`, additive and drawn over everything) that jump out of line, with bands that slide or drop out; vertical light streaks and grid-snapped blocks; pale cyan Wingdings glyphs, gold embers and dark shards drifting up; a violet light; at full strength the character blinking out for a frame, and a low drone. Its levels are `Off`, `Hint` (a frame of the split every few seconds), `Lingering` (a faint steady shimmer) and `Full`. The cutscene `aura` step sets it on an NPC, and the cutscene clears any aura it set when it ends. Reduced Motion keeps a still outline with no jumps, flicker, streaks or blocks.

For testing, **Lumenfall > Secrets > Glitch Aura** sets an editor-only preview level on Marigold (kept in the editor's PlayerPrefs; builds never show it), and **Go To Dorm Reception** takes the player to her in Play Mode. The preview can only raise the level the story sets, never lower it. Set it back to **Off** when done. **Set Up Key Glitch Scene** skips a loaded game to the end of the key hunt (every favour done, all four dormmates asked, the inner voice heard) and takes the player to reception, so talking to Marigold plays the glitch; use a throwaway save.

The story lines (`Resources/GameEvents/settling_in.json`) are placeholders, to be rewritten.

## Secrets

Deltarune-style secrets: odd actions, hinted at in dialogue, that lead somewhere pointless and charming. Each step is a saved flag (`secret.*`), so a secret half done stays half done. Three of them open a small room built in code by `SecretRooms`, like Orio's legacy hall: the loader resolves it by map id, so a save made inside restores. Each room has one person to talk to, whose first conversation hands over a curio, and a glowing ring by the door that takes the player back to where they came from. After that, their `chat_<id>` offers the curio again until it has been taken. Their dialogue is in `Resources/GameEvents/secrets.json`; the furniture and the janitor's model are referenced by `Resources/SecretRoomArt.asset`. - **Quiet rooms (`SecretRoomMood`):** the forgotten room (the egg) and the dog's room play no music (`AudioContextPolicy.MapMusicCue`) and swap the scene's ambient light, fog and camera sky for their own while the player is inside, putting all of it back on leaving. Their sounds are generated in code (`SecretRoomAudio`). Things worth a look show a **Look** prompt and play a narrated line (`RoomInspectable`, dialogues `inspect_*` in `secrets.json`, listed in `SecretRooms.InspectDialogues`); they yield to anyone in reach to talk to. **The forgotten room** is a long, dark corridor (5 by 15): a worn runner, panelled walls lined with empty frames (two crooked), a coat rack with only a small dog's lead on it, an armchair facing the far wall, and pendant lamps leaving pools of light (the first dead, the second failing and clicking), with dust turning in them. The building hums and an unseen clock ticks, and the clock stops while the man talks. Once he has spoken and the player walks back past row 8, every lamp fails at once; when they come back he is gone for good (`secret.forgotten.gone`) and so is the clock. If he still had the egg, it stays on its stand and can be taken (`secret_forgotten_egg`). Lost Echoes from beside him brings the faded photograph behind him back for a moment: a man with a stick and a small white dog. **The dog's room** (9 by 7) is white without edges: bright ambient light, white fog and a white sky wash its walls out. The dog breathes in time with its snoring, twitches an ear, thumps its tail, and sleeps under a loading spinner; around it are the north gate's chewed-up loading bar, its bowl, and a pink-and-black missing-texture hole in the floor. Vis near it floats it (still asleep), Rotara turns it all the way round, and Lost Echoes shows paw prints from the hole to its bed. The rooms take their mood from the game's own secrets; nothing in them is borrowed art, music or sound.
- **Spells:** the spell secrets listen to `SpellCaster.CastAttempted`, raised for every cast from input before the spell looks for a target. A handler that answers it sets `Handled`, and the spell does nothing else (Lost Echoes still searches).
- **Doors:** the knock listens to `BuildingSignInteraction.Checked` and `Closed`, and swaps door 4's text for that visit with `ShowInstead`. The staff closet adds itself to the main hall door (`P.C03`) when the campus loads, as an `IBlockedPortalHandler`.
- **Out of order (`SequenceBreakEnding`):** the story breaks in two deliberately obscure ways. Finishing a secret without having heard the hints that lead to it, which only someone who has played before would know to do (`SequenceBreakEnding.HintsFor`): the devs' room needs Stella met (her pi line is in her introduction), the Lantern Bearer needs Luna's and Moss's lantern lines, and the staff closet needs one of Juno's gossip hints and Voltaire's shelf line, each recorded by a `hint.heard.*` flag on the line. The dog's hints are the gate's own notices, and the hidden curios never count (the dorm knock answers "How do you know that knock?" instead of opening), plays `secret_sequence_break_knowledge`; solving all six Chapter 1 puzzles before ever setting foot in the dormitory (`story.dorm.visited`, set on entering it) plays `secret_sequence_break`. Solving one puzzle early is fine. Either way, once nothing else is on screen the glitch plays and a still of the game is captured. The screen then cuts to black, all sound pauses and time stops, and the voice's own music (`Resources/Audio/Music/sequence_break`, `SequenceBreakEnding.VoiceMusicPath`) starts with it. A voice that speaks in hands (a W. D. Gaster nod) says "Interesting." one slow letter at a time, then seven lines in the real Wingdings font (`WingdingsText`: plain English drawn in the system's installed Wingdings, so they decode; where Wingdings is missing, the capitals fall back to the Unicode symbols Wingdings draws), then "TIME FOR ME TO HAVE SOME FUN" and "BREAKING YOU", shaking one letter at a time. The lines are the same for both causes and live in `SequenceBreakEnding.Lines`. The still comes back and a pale hand reaches in from each side (`ScreenCrush`: PolyOne Studio's rigged pair, `SecretRoomArt.crushingHand`, CC BY 4.0 and credited in the repository README). The palms press its edges and three shoves squeeze it narrower, bowing it toward the player while cracks spread from both palms with a white impact flash each time, as the original glass break's blows had. The recorded break (`Resources/Audio/SoundEffects/lumen_break`, `SequenceBreakEnding.BreakSoundPath`) plays from the first crack in place of the effect's own snaps, which only play if it is missing. Then the hands rush together, and the moment they meet the screen breaks between them in the brightest flash. Its glass bursts out from between the palms in pieces cut like the original glass break's, each with a bright edge, and they tumble down into the dark while the hands draw apart and away. The game closes once the last piece has fallen and the break sound has finished, the screen held black meanwhile (play mode ends in the editor). Without the hand model the still cracks and shatters instead (`ScreenShatter`), then the game closes. **Lumenfall > Secrets > Sequence Break > Render Hand Crush Frames** renders key moments to `Logs/HandCrush` without playing the ending. The moment the screen goes black, the run is reset as New Game does and saved over the autosave and the manual slot the run came from, so the file the player had now holds the very beginning (the one deliberate exception to automatic saving never writing a manual slot). That slot is the linked one or, for a run continued from the autosave (which drops the Quick Save link), `ManualSaveSlot.Origin`: the `save.origin-slot` counter, set whenever the run is started in, loaded from or saved to a slot, and kept when the autosave is loaded. Only a run that never had a slot erases just the autosave. The game is also locked out (`SequenceBreakLockout`, the `secret.sequence_break.locked_out` PlayerPref, outside every save slot), so closing it during the black screen escapes nothing. While locked out, every launch opens the title as if nothing had happened, but to its own track (`Resources/Audio/Music/lumen_MMMMMMMMMMMMMMMMMMMM`, `SequenceBreakLockout.TitleMusicPath`) and answering no key or click. When that track ends the screen cuts to black, and nothing of the title shows through: 30 seconds of black and silence, then Miki (the devs' room model, in a flickering spotlight) asks if the player wants to play again. No closes the game, and the next launch repeats. Yes means giving something in return; no to that also closes the game, and yes lets Miki keep it (never named; `secret.sequence_break.given` is set), lifts the lockout, and the title, open under the black all along, answers again with its usual music. Loading the reset autosave plays the New Game opening again (`secret.sequence_break.opening_pending`). For testing, **Lumenfall > Secrets > Sequence Break** locks out the next launch or clears the lockout.

## Story-only characters

`StoryNpc` spawns a character from their NPC catalog record (prefab, map and cell) instead of baking them into an area prefab, in the way the ice trial spawns its Cinna. Each frame, the owning scene says whether the story wants them present.

- They appear on their map as a child of its area object, facing south, with a `DynamicNpc` and a one-cell `GridObstacle`. They switch off with the area, and are removed when they leave their map or are no longer wanted.
- They are removed while the startup menu is open, because saves load then, and a blocker on a saved player cell made the world provider reject the save.
- If the player stands on their cell, they wait until the player steps off.
- If their authored cell is blocked (a tree, a wall, the fountain) or is a doorway, they stand on the nearest free cell within four cells instead. The interior and forest layouts only exist in the area prefabs, so authored cells cannot all be checked ahead of time.
